# The Coffee License: Arguments For Validity and Enforceability

*A legal analysis supporting The Coffee License as a valid, enforceable licensing model with strong precedent in copyright and contract law*

---

## Executive Summary

The Coffee License represents a legally sound evolution of established licensing practices. Built on the rock-solid foundation of the MIT/BSD license family, it incorporates tiered commercial licensing—a model with decades of successful enforcement. This document demonstrates why The Coffee License is both legally valid and practically enforceable.

---

## Part I: Legal Foundation and Validity

### 1. Copyright Holder's Absolute Right to Set Terms

#### The Bedrock Principle
Copyright law grants creators **exclusive rights** to their work. 17 U.S.C. § 106 provides copyright holders the exclusive right to:
- Reproduce the work
- Distribute copies
- Create derivative works
- Publicly display the work

**The copyright holder can license these rights on ANY terms they choose.** This is not controversial—it's black-letter law recognized in every jurisdiction with copyright protection.

#### Precedent: Conditional Licensing
Courts have consistently upheld conditional licenses:
- **Jacobsen v. Katzer (Fed. Cir. 2008)**: Open source license conditions are enforceable copyright restrictions
- **Artifex Software v. Hancom (N.D. Cal. 2017)**: GPL conditions enforced against commercial violator
- **Software Freedom Conservancy v. Vizio (Cal. 2022)**: Confirmed third-party enforcement rights

The Coffee License's conditions (payment above threshold) are no different than GPL's conditions (provide source code) or Creative Commons' conditions (provide attribution).

### 2. Tiered Licensing Is Industry Standard

#### Commercial Precedent
The software industry has used tiered licensing for decades:

**By Revenue:**
- Elastic License (revenue thresholds)
- Various SaaS licenses

**By Employee Count:**
- Microsoft Volume Licensing
- Atlassian pricing tiers
- JetBrains educational/commercial split

**By Entity Type:**
- Unity's revenue-based licensing
- Unreal Engine's royalty structure
- Oracle's processor-based licensing

**By Explicit Dual Licensing:**
- MySQL (GPL + Commercial)
- Qt (LGPL + Commercial)
- MongoDB (SSPL + Commercial)

The Coffee License's net worth threshold is simply another axis for tiered licensing—one that's arguably more equitable than alternatives.

### 3. Net Worth Thresholds Have Legal Recognition

#### SEC Regulations
The Securities and Exchange Commission uses net worth thresholds extensively:
- **Accredited Investor**: $1M net worth or $200K income
- **Qualified Purchaser**: $5M+ in investments
- **Regulation D exemptions**: Based on company net worth

These thresholds are routinely enforced despite similar measurement challenges.

#### Tax Law
Tax codes worldwide use net worth/asset thresholds:
- Estate tax exemptions
- Corporate tax brackets
- Wealth taxes (where applicable)

If governments can enforce net worth thresholds for taxation, private parties can enforce them for licensing.

#### Contract Law
Commercial contracts routinely include financial triggers:
- Earnout provisions in M&A
- Revenue sharing arrangements
- Performance-based pricing

### 4. "Open Source" Is Not a Legal Term

#### No Legal Definition
"Open source" is a **trademark** of the Open Source Initiative—not a legal category. Using a different licensing model doesn't invalidate the license; it simply means it may not use OSI's trademark.

#### Semantic Argument Is Irrelevant
Whether The Coffee License is "open source" has no bearing on whether it's:
- A valid copyright license
- An enforceable contract
- A legal way to distribute software

Many successful projects use non-OSI-approved licenses:
- **Elastic License 2.0**: Powers Elasticsearch
- **Business Source License**: Used by MariaDB, CockroachDB, Sentry
- **Server Side Public License**: MongoDB
- **PolyForm licenses**: Growing adoption

### 5. Based on Proven MIT License Framework

The Coffee License's terms are directly derived from the MIT License—one of the most battle-tested licenses in software history:

**Identical provisions:**
- Grant of rights (use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, sell)
- Permission to sublicense
- Attribution requirements
- Warranty disclaimer
- Limitation of liability

The only addition is the conditional tier for large entities. The core legal machinery is identical to MIT, which has withstood 35+ years of legal scrutiny.

## Part II: Enforceability Arguments

### 1. Clear Contract Formation

#### Acceptance Through Use
Software licenses are routinely enforced despite no physical signature. Courts recognize:
- **Shrinkwrap licenses**: Enforced since ProCD v. Zeidenberg (7th Cir. 1996)
- **Clickwrap licenses**: Standard in all software
- **Browsewrap licenses**: Enforced with conspicuous notice

**Open source licenses** create contracts through use:
> "By copying, modifying, or distributing the Work, you accept this License." (GPLv3)

The Coffee License operates identically—use constitutes acceptance.

#### Consideration Exists
Consideration is present on both sides:
- **Licensor provides**: Valuable software, permission to use
- **Licensee provides**: Compliance with conditions, attribution

This is the same consideration structure as MIT, GPL, and Apache licenses—all routinely enforced.

### 2. Terms Are Sufficiently Definite

#### Net Worth Is Determinable
Companies know their net worth. They calculate it for:
- Financial statements (required by law for public companies)
- Tax filings
- Insurance purposes
- Loan applications
- M&A transactions

The objection that "companies don't know their net worth" is specious—every CFO knows this number.

#### Standard Accounting Applies
When terms aren't explicitly defined, courts apply:
- Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)
- International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)
- Industry-standard definitions

This is routine in commercial contracts. Courts regularly interpret undefined financial terms using standard accounting.

#### Reasonable Interpretation Standard
Contract law applies the "reasonable person" standard. A reasonable person would understand:
- "Net worth" = Total assets minus total liabilities
- "Market cap" = Share price × shares outstanding
- Measured at time of first use

### 3. Threshold Is Clear and Binary

#### $500M Is Unambiguous
The threshold is:
- A specific number
- In a specific currency
- With a clear determination method

Compare this to deliberately vague terms courts enforce routinely:
- "Reasonable efforts"
- "Material adverse change"
- "Commercially reasonable"

$500M net worth is far more precise than terms in most commercial contracts.

#### Self-Selection Works
In practice, entities know which tier they fall into:
- **Individuals**: Obviously below
- **Startups**: Obviously below
- **SMBs**: Obviously below
- **Fortune 500 companies**: Obviously above

The edge cases (companies near $500M) can easily verify with their finance team.

### 4. Enforcement Mechanisms Exist

#### Copyright Infringement Remedies
If a mega-corp uses the software without the commercial license, they're committing **copyright infringement**. Available remedies:

**Statutory Damages**: Up to $150,000 per work for willful infringement (17 U.S.C. § 504)
**Actual Damages**: Lost licensing fees plus infringer's profits
**Injunctive Relief**: Court orders to cease use
**Attorney's Fees**: Prevailing party can recover costs

#### The $50 Fee Is Not the Measure of Damages
The license fee is merely the price of compliance. Damages for infringement are measured by:
- Statutory damages (regardless of fee)
- Actual harm to the market
- Unjust enrichment of infringer

A company that builds a product on unlicensed software faces damages far exceeding $50.

### 5. Jurisdictional Issues Are Standard

#### Choice of Law Rules Apply
Every cross-border contract faces jurisdictional questions. Courts resolve them using:
- Choice of law provisions (which can be added)
- Most significant relationship test
- Governmental interest analysis

This is not unique to The Coffee License—it's inherent in all software licensing.

#### Venue Is Determinable
Without a choice of forum clause, standard rules apply:
- Where defendant resides
- Where breach occurred
- Where contract was formed

Courts handle these issues daily in software disputes.

### 6. The $50 Fee Makes Litigation Irrational—That's the Point

#### Compliance Is Easier Than Fighting
The genius of the low fee is that it makes compliance the only rational choice:

**Cost to comply**: $50 + email
**Cost to fight**: $50,000+ in legal fees (minimum)

No general counsel will authorize $50,000 to avoid paying $50. The fee is deliberately set below the threshold of resistance.

#### It's Not About Revenue
The purpose isn't to collect $50—it's to:
- Force acknowledgment of the developer
- Create a relationship
- Establish that terms apply

The enforceability comes from the copyright infringement remedies, not the fee amount.

## Part III: Addressing Counterarguments

### 1. "It Discriminates Based on Size"

**Response**: Copyright holders can absolutely discriminate in licensing. Examples:
- Educational discounts (discriminate against commercial users)
- Geographic pricing (discriminate by location)
- Non-profit licenses (discriminate by entity type)
- Personal vs. commercial licenses (discriminate by use)

The OSD's non-discrimination clause applies to what OSI calls "open source"—not to all software licensing.

### 2. "Net Worth Is Ambiguous"

**Response**: Less ambiguous than terms courts enforce daily:
- "Best efforts" vs. "reasonable efforts"
- "Material breach"
- "Force majeure"
- "Good faith"

Financial thresholds with dollar amounts are among the **most** definite terms in contract law.

### 3. "Measurement Timing Is Unclear"

**Response**: Standard contract interpretation applies:
- Measured at time of first use (most reasonable reading)
- Alternatively, at time of distribution/deployment
- If genuinely ambiguous, contra proferentem applies—interpreted against drafter

This is easily clarified with one sentence if desired, but courts can resolve it regardless.

### 4. "No Enforcement Mechanism Exists"

**Response**: The same "enforcement mechanism" as every other open source license:
- Developer discovers violation
- Developer sends cease and desist
- If ignored, developer files lawsuit
- Copyright infringement damages apply

No open source license has built-in enforcement. They all rely on the same legal system.

### 5. "Companies Can Evade Through Subsidiaries"

**Response**: Corporate veil piercing and alter ego doctrines address this:
- Courts look to economic reality
- Shell companies don't defeat obligations
- Parent company liability exists

A subsidiary of Google using software doesn't magically become a small company.

## Part IV: Supporting Policy Arguments

### 1. Promotes Economic Justice

The license acknowledges economic reality:
- Individual developers create enormous value
- Mega-corporations extract that value without compensation
- Asymmetry of power distorts the market

Tiered licensing based on ability to pay is **more equitable** than flat licensing.

### 2. Follows Precedent in Physical Goods

Tiered pricing is standard in the physical world:
- Progressive taxation
- Sliding scale medical fees
- Means-tested government services
- Student/senior discounts

Software licensing is merely catching up.

### 3. Builds Sustainable Open Source

The current open source model is failing:
- Developer burnout is epidemic
- Critical infrastructure is undermaintained
- Tragedy of the commons destroys projects

The Coffee License offers a path to sustainability while maintaining free access for those who need it most.

### 4. Corporate Legal Prefers Clear Terms

Counter-intuitively, corporate legal teams often prefer:
- Clear obligations over ambiguous freedoms
- Known costs over uncertain liability
- Definite terms over legal gray areas

A $50 fee with clear terms is preferable to MIT's complete ambiguity about liability.

## Part V: Practical Enforceability

### 1. Reputation-Based Enforcement

Even without litigation, the license creates:
- PR risk for non-compliant mega-corps
- "Name and shame" potential
- ESG/corporate responsibility pressure

The court of public opinion often moves faster than courts of law.

### 2. Community Enforcement

The open source community can:
- Identify violations through code review
- Report violations to developers
- Amplify non-compliance publicly

This is identical to how GPL violations are discovered and addressed.

### 3. Gradual Adoption Creates Precedent

As more projects adopt The Coffee License:
- Best practices will emerge
- Template commercial licenses will develop
- Case law will establish interpretations
- Enforcement infrastructure will grow

Every successful license started with zero precedent.

## Conclusion

The Coffee License is built on unshakeable legal foundations:

1. **Copyright holders have absolute right to set licensing terms**
2. **Tiered/conditional licensing is standard industry practice**
3. **Net worth thresholds have decades of legal recognition**
4. **The MIT License framework is battle-tested**
5. **Copyright infringement provides robust remedies**

The license is enforceable because it's simply a conditional copyright license—the same legal structure underlying every dual-licensed project. The conditions are clearer than those in most commercial contracts, and the remedies for violation are the powerful tools of copyright law.

Developers can adopt The Coffee License with confidence that:
- Their copyright is protected
- Their conditions are enforceable
- Mega-corporations face real legal exposure for non-compliance
- The license will withstand legal scrutiny

The Coffee License represents not radical innovation, but thoughtful evolution of proven licensing models. It deserves serious consideration from any developer seeking to balance openness with sustainability.
